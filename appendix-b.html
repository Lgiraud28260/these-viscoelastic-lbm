Appendix B

Introduction of Objective Derivatives

In Appendix A, we found that the derivatives of second-order tensors we had to consider in the constitutive equations were necessarily of the objective type, that is, these derivatives must be independent of the motion of the observer. The introduction of objective derivatives into the constitutive equation of our fluid requires the introduction of nonlinear terms in the equilibrium quantities. But we must first make the following remark; since the beginning of our study, we have considered that the stress tensor we were manipulating was a symmetric traceless tensor. However, among the objective derivatives, the convective derivative does not preserve the trace. It cannot therefore be integrated in our models because we cannot, in their current state, represent tensors having a non-zero trace. On the other hand, the Jaumann derivative is the only convective derivative that preserves the trace and is given by: \[\begin{equation} {\mathcal{W}}_{[1]}^J={D\,{\mathcal{W}}\over D t}+{1\over 2}\left({\mathcal{W}}\cdot\Omega+\Omega^T \cdot{\mathcal{W}}\right). \end{equation}\]

It is then easy to demonstrate that, to obtain Jaumann derivatives in the constitutive equation, it is necessary to also introduce Jaumann derivatives into the conservation and pseudo-conservation equations of our models. Thus, for the eleven-velocity model, these five equations were, according to chapter 5 of the last part of this manuscript: \[\begin{eqnarray} &&\hspace{-0.5in}\partial_t\rho\,+\,\partial_xj_x+\partial_yj_y=0,\nonumber\\ &&\hspace{-0.5in}\partial_tj_x+\partial_x\,(c_s^2\rho)+c_{\perp}^2\,\left(1-\frac{\lambda_\gamma}{2} \right)\left( \partial_x H_{xx}+\partial_yH_{xy}\right)=\nu_\infty\,\Delta j_x+\xi_\infty\,\partial_x\left(\partial_xj_x+\partial_yj_y \right)\nonumber\\ &&\hspace{-0.5in}\partial_tj_y+\partial_y\,(c_s^2\rho)+c_{\perp}^2\,\left(1-\frac{\lambda_\gamma}{2} \right)\left( \partial_xH_{xy}-\partial_yH_{xx}\right)=\nu_\infty\,\Delta j_y+\xi_\infty\,\partial_y\left(\partial_xj_x+\partial_yj_y \right)\nonumber\\ &&\hspace{-0.5in} H_{xx}+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,\partial_tH_{xx}+\rho\,\frac{1}{\lambda_\gamma}{\dot D}_{xx}^*- {D_\infty \over \lambda_\gamma}\,\Delta H_{xx}=0, \nonumber\\ &&\hspace{-0.5in} H_{xy}+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,\partial_tH_{xy}+\rho\,\frac{1}{\lambda_\gamma}{\dot D}_{xy}^*- {D_\infty \over \lambda_\gamma} \,\Delta H_{xy}=0. \nonumber \end{eqnarray}\] To obtain these equations, we had chosen: \[\begin{eqnarray} &&S^{(0)}_{xx}=0,\qquad\quad S^{(0)}_{xy}=0,\quad e^{(0)}=c_s^2\rho\nonumber\\ &&q^{(0)}_x=-\frac{j_x}{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad q^{(0)}_y=-\frac{j_y}{2}.\nonumber \end{eqnarray}\]

If we now take: \[\begin{eqnarray} &\displaystyle S^{(0)}_{xx}=\frac{j_x^2-j_y^2}{\rho(1-4\,c_{\perp}^2)},&\displaystyle S^{(0)}_{xy}=\frac{j_xj_y}{\rho(1-2\,c_{\perp}^2)},\qquad \displaystyle e^{(0)}=c_s^2\rho+\frac{(j_x^2+j_y^2)}{2\rho},\nonumber\\ &\hspace{-0.5in}\displaystyle q^{(0)}_x=-\frac{j_x}{2}-\frac{3}{2}\,\left(1-\frac{\lambda_\gamma}{2} \right)\,\left(\frac{j_xH_{xx}}{\rho}-\frac{j_y H_{xy}}{\rho} \right)&\mbox{and }\displaystyle q^{(0)}_y=-\frac{j_y}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\,\left(1-\frac{\lambda_\gamma}{2} \right)\,\left(\frac{j_xH_{xy}}{\rho}+\frac{j_yH_{xx}}{\rho} \right),\nonumber \end{eqnarray}\] we then obtain: \[\begin{eqnarray} &&\hspace{-0.5in}{D\over Dt}\rho+\rho \left(\partial_x{j_x\over \rho}+\partial_y{j_y\over \rho}\right)=0,\nonumber\\ &&\hspace{-0.5in}{D\over Dt}j_x+\partial_x\,(c_s^2\rho)+c_{\perp}^2\,\left(1-\frac{\lambda_\gamma}{2} \right)\left( \partial_x H_{xx}+\partial_yH_{xy}\right)=\nu_\infty\,\Delta j_x+\xi_\infty\,\partial_x\left(\partial_xj_x+\partial_yj_y \right)\nonumber\\ &&\hspace{-0.5in}{D\over Dt}j_y+\partial_y\,(c_s^2\rho)+c_{\perp}^2\,\left(1-\frac{\lambda_\gamma}{2} \right)\left( \partial_xH_{xy}-\partial_yH_{xx}\right)=\nu_\infty\,\Delta j_y+\xi_\infty\,\partial_y\left(\partial_xj_x+\partial_yj_y \right)\nonumber\\ &&\hspace{-0.5in} H_{xx}+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,{D\over Dt}H_{xx}+\rho\,\frac{1}{\lambda_\gamma}{\dot D}_{xx}^*- {D_\infty \over \lambda_\gamma}\,\Delta H_{xx}+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,\left(\partial_y\frac{j_xH_{xy}}{\rho} -\partial_x\frac{j_yH_{xy}}{\rho} \right)=0, \nonumber\\ &&\hspace{-0.5in} H_{xy}+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,{D\over Dt}H_{xy}+\rho\,\frac{1}{\lambda_\gamma}{\dot D}_{xy}^*- {D_\infty \over \lambda_\gamma} \,\Delta H_{xy}+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,\left(\partial_x\frac{j_yH_{xx}}{\rho}-\partial_y\frac{j_xH_{xx}}{\rho}\right)=0. \nonumber \end{eqnarray}\]

We have thus introduced a material derivative in the equations, but we have also introduced additional terms, which turn out to be isotropic, and which will couple nonlinearly the two modes \(H_{xx}\) and \(H_{xy}\). The idea here is to give a non-zero equilibrium value to the new modes of type \(P\). But no solution has been found to replace these terms by those of the Jaumann derivative; the number of free parameters of the system is insufficient.

On the other hand, it is possible to do so in the fifteen-velocity model, by choosing the following equilibrium values and taking up again the notations we had used at that time: \[\begin{eqnarray} &\hspace{-0.5in}\displaystyle S^{(0)}_{xx}=0,&\displaystyle S^{(0)}_{xy}=0,\qquad \displaystyle e^{(0)}=c_s^2\rho+\frac{(j_x^2+j_y^2)}{2\rho},\nonumber\\ &\hspace{-0.5in}\displaystyle q_{x1}^{(0)}=\kappa_1j_x-{1\over 3}\,\left(1-\frac{\lambda_\gamma}{2} \right)\,\left(-11\frac{j_xP_{xx}}{\rho}+2\frac{j_y P_{xy}}{\rho} \right),&\displaystyle q_{y1}^{(0)}=\kappa_1j_y-{1 \over 3}\,\left(1-\frac{\lambda_\gamma}{2} \right)\,\left(2\frac{j_x P_{xy}}{\rho}+11\frac{j_yP_{xx}}{\rho} \right), \nonumber\nonumber\\ &\hspace{-0.5in}\displaystyle q_{x2}^{(0)}=\kappa_2j_x+{7\over 6}\,\left(1-\frac{\lambda_\gamma}{2} \right)\,\left(\frac{j_xP_{xx}}{\rho}-\frac{j_y P_{xy}}{\rho} \right),&\displaystyle q_{y2}^{(0)}=\kappa_2j_y-{7\over 6}\,\left(1-\frac{\lambda_\gamma}{2} \right)\,\left(\frac{j_x P_{xy}}{\rho}+\frac{j_yP_{xx}}{\rho} \right),\nonumber\\ &\hspace{-0.5in}\displaystyle H_{xx}^{(0)}={1\over 2c_{\perp}^2\,\left(1-\frac{\lambda_\gamma}{2} \right)}\left(j_x^2-j_y^2\right),&\mbox{and }\quad\displaystyle H_{xy}^{(0)}={1\over c_{\perp}^2\,\left(1-\frac{\lambda_\gamma}{2} \right)}j_x\,j_y,\nonumber \end{eqnarray}\] with: \[\begin{eqnarray} &&H_{xx}=\kappa\,P_{xx},\nonumber\\ &&H_{xy}=\kappa\,P_{xy},\nonumber \end{eqnarray}\] the dynamical equations become: \[\begin{eqnarray} &&\hspace{-0.5in}{D\over Dt}\rho+\rho \left(\partial_x{j_x\over \rho}+\partial_y{j_y\over \rho}\right)=0,\nonumber\\ &&\hspace{-0.5in}{D\over Dt}j_x+\partial_x\,(c_s^2\rho)+c_{\perp}^2\,\left(1-\frac{\lambda_\gamma}{2} \right)\left( \partial_x H_{xx}+\partial_yH_{xy}\right)=\nu_\infty\,\Delta j_x+\xi_\infty\,\partial_x\left(\partial_xj_x+\partial_yj_y \right)\nonumber\\ &&\hspace{-0.5in}{D\over Dt}j_y+\partial_y\,(c_s^2\rho)+c_{\perp}^2\,\left(1-\frac{\lambda_\gamma}{2} \right)\left( \partial_xH_{xy}-\partial_yH_{xx}\right)=\nu_\infty\,\Delta j_y+\xi_\infty\,\partial_y\left(\partial_xj_x+\partial_yj_y \right)\nonumber\\ &&\hspace{-0.5in} H_{xx}+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\, H_{xx\,[1]}^J +\rho\,\frac{1}{\lambda_\gamma}{\dot D}_{xx}^*- {D_\infty \over \lambda_\gamma}\,\Delta H_{xx}=0, \nonumber\\ &&\hspace{-0.5in} H_{xy}+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,H_{xy\,[1]}^J +\rho\,\frac{1}{\lambda_\gamma}{\dot D}_{xy}^*- {D_\infty \over \lambda_\gamma} \,\Delta H_{xy}=0. \nonumber \end{eqnarray}\]

We then have as constitutive equation of our fluid: \[\begin{equation} {\mathbf{\sigma}}+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,{\mathbf{\sigma}}_{[1]}^J =-\rho\, \nu_0 \left({\dot{\mathbf{D}}}^*+\frac{\nu_\infty}{\nu_0} \,\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\, {\mathbf{\dot D}}^{*J}_{[1]} \right)\,+\,\frac{D_\infty}{\lambda_\gamma}\,\Delta {\mathbf{\sigma} }. \end{equation}\]

In conclusion, the eleven-velocity model does not allow modeling Jaumann derivatives; on the other hand, the fifteen-velocity model has sufficient free parameters to model this type of derivative.
