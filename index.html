Abstract

In this thesis, we first present the Lattice Boltzmann method as a relaxation method of particle populations towards an equilibrium state.

Then, in a second part, we study the impact of the relaxation times of these populations on the introduction of boundary conditions simulating solid walls. We deduce a law allowing the choice of these relaxation times so that the effective position of the walls no longer depends on them.

Next, by doubling the number of moments required for the introduction of viscous effects and giving them very long relaxation times, we construct two isotropic models allowing the simulation of Jeffreys-type viscoelastic fluids. Many numerical simulations have validated this kind of behavior.

Finally, in a last part, we introduce a variant of the Chapman-Enskog expansion. It allows us to exhibit a constitutive equation for our fluid. This law turns out, moreover, to be very close to that of a Jeffreys-type viscoelastic fluid.

We also give research directions for introducing convective derivatives, as well as for introducing viscosity effects varying with shear.

Keywords: Lattice Boltzmann Equation, Viscoelasticity, Numerical Method, Multi-scale Technique, Jeffreys Fluids, Chapman-Enskog Expansion.
