Part III · Modeling of Viscoelasticity with the Lattice Boltzmann Method

Introduction

Attempts at the numerical simulation of viscoelastic fluids, or more generally of fluids having an elastic character, carried out over the past twenty years, have shown all the difficulty of the undertaking. Indeed, the nonlinear coupling between the constitutive equation and the momentum conservation equation proves to be of great complexity. Industrial flows require robust numerical methods, capable of adapting to real needs of simulating fluids with complex characters moving in complex geometries. The three main families of existing simulation methods that seem suited to the task, namely finite elements, finite differences and spectral methods, share a common approach, namely to discretize the differential equations describing the fluid flow.

The lattice Boltzmann method for the modelling of viscoelastic fluids, which we are going to introduce here, has a particle-like approach. Indeed, in the same way as when the first lattice gas was introduced, we will idealize the behaviour of populations of particles that constitute the viscoelastic fluid. Then, by an expansion or by successive approximations, we will deduce the macroscopic behaviour equations. The advantage of such a modelling is then obvious: we no longer have to solve any system of coupled differential equations, but only to evolve a set of populations on a lattice with simple collision and propagation rules. At the same time we inherit a crucial advantage of the lattice Boltzmann method, namely the ease of introducing boundary conditions that allow the representation of complex geometries, as well as the “natural” parallelization of cellular-automaton-type algorithms.
